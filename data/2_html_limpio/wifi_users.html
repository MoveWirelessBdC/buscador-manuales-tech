WiFi Users Statistics

The WiFi Users statistics provide information about the users who log in to your WiFi. To access them, go to the Reporting -> Statistics section and select WiFi Users from the upper bar.

Note: The table loading time depends on several factors, including selected date range, amount of data which must be aggregated, or your internet connection speed. The average display time should be less than 5 min, and should not exceed 10 min. In case it is higher, try reloading the page, or change the date range. If the data is still not displayed, contact your administrator.

To ensure the best system performance, part of the data is stored in probabilistic structures, therefore the expected error in relation to real data can be up to 0.1%.

These statistics can be viewed for a specified time frame; to define it, select the time range in the Time Picker located at the top of the Statistics panel, and click Show to confirm your selection.

The What Do You Know About Your WiFi Users? Section

New Users

The New Users table displays the number of users who logged in to your WiFi for the first time.

Returning New Users

The Returning New Users table presents the number of Returning New Users.

A Returning New User is a person who has been a New User within the designated time frame and then returned and logged in again within this period, which resulted in classifying them as both New and Returning one.

Example: Given the time frame is set from 01/01 to 07/01, when a new user logs in to the WiFi on 01/01, and then returns on 04/01 and logs in again, they are classified as both the New User and the Returning New User for this time frame.

Returning Users

The Returning Users table presents the number of Returning Users within the selected time frame.

A Returning User is a user who has logged in to your WiFi an additional time in the designated period.

All Users

The All Users table displays the total number of Logged-In Users. It is the sum of the New Users and Returning Users minus the number of Returning New Users.

Average User Count per Hour

The Average User Count per Hour bar chart shows the average number of users who have logged in to your WiFi. These data are displayed as a total number of New and Returning Users per hour, and broken down by these two types.

User Count

The User Count bar chart presents the total number of users who have logged in to your WiFi. These data are displayed as a total number of New and Returning Users per day, and broken down by these two types.

Login Methods over Time

The Login Methods Over Time bar chart displays the total number of users who have logged in to your WiFi, broken by login methods which they have chosen to log in to your network. You can display them for three types of granularity: hour, day, or week.

Login Methods

The Login Methods doughnut chart shows the percentage breakdown of the login methods which have been selected by the users during the login process.

Rush Hours

The Rush Hours graph is a heat map which displays the average number of users per hour, in colours; the higher the number of Logged-In Users, the darker the shade of the given field. It is a visualisation of daytime traffic at your venue which allows you to identify the time of day when the user congestion is at its peak.

To view the number of users at a specific time, hover your mouse over the appropriate area.

Best Hour

The Best Hour table displays the day and time when the number of the Logged-In Users was the highest.

Best Day

The Best Day table displays the day when the number of the Logged-In Users was the highest.

Best Day on Record

The Best Day on Record table displays the date when the number of the Logged-In Users was the highest in history.

The Which Suborganisation Does the Data Come From? Section

Users per Organisation

The Users per Organisation doughnut chart presents the percentage breakdown of Sub-organisations which have contributed to the collection of all data.

Participation Rate

The Participation Rate table presents the percentage breakdown of all Sub-organisations which have contributed to the collection of all data, supplemented with the breakdowns of those Sub-organisations by:

  • the number of WiFi users from which the data has been collected,
  • the percentage breakdown of the total number of users in the Organisation,
  • the total data transfer,
  • the total duration of user sessions.

The Who Are Your Users? Section

Country of Origin

The Country of Origin graph is a heat map which presents the users’ countries of origin. It is complemented with the bar chart which displays the percentage breakdown of countries and the total numbers of the Logged-In users. These data is calculated based on the language of the browser which they have used to log in to the WiFi.

To view the number of users, hover your mouse over either the map or the bar chart.

Language

The Language doughnut chart displays the percentage breakdown of the users' languages based on the language of the browser which they have used to log in to the WiFi.

Gender

The Gender pie chart presents the percentage breakdown of users by gender.

This data can be collected during the login process through the Forms (provided that the gender option has been included in the Form and the users have declared their gender in it), or through the Social Media logins.

To preview the exact percentage of users of a specific gender, hover your mouse over the desired part of the chart.

Age

The Age bar chart presents the number of customers broken by their age. To preview the exact number of users in a specific age range, hover your mouse over the desired bar.

This data can be collected during the login process through the Forms (provided that the age option has been included in the Form and the users have declared their age in it), or through the Social Media logins.

The What Do You Know About Your Users' Devices? Section

Unique Devices

The Unique Devices table presents how many individual devices connected to your WiFi and completed the login process

What is a Unique Device?

The user’s device is identified by its MAC Address, which is an individual device ID. When the user connects to your network, the MAC of their device is registered in the system. From now on, whenever the user connects to the network in a designated period, their device will still count as 1.

Average Number of Devices

The Average Number of Devices table shows the average daily number of unique devices which have logged in to your WiFi.

It is the sum of the number of connections of all unique devices, which occurred per day in a designated period, divided by the number of these days.

Devices

The ** Devices ** pie chart shows the percentage breakdown of device types where users logged into your WiFi network. It is calculated as the sum of sessions which have started in a given period, broken down by type of device, and converted into percentages.

Operating Systems

The Operating Systems pie chart shows the percentage breakdown of the operating systems of the devices which are used to connect to your WiFi. It is calculated as the sum of sessions which have started in a given period, broken down by type of operating system, and converted into percentages.

Number of Devices

The Number of Devices bar chart presents how many devices connected to your network per selected granularity (hour, day or week). To preview the exact number of devices, hover your mouse over the desired bar.

The How Devices Use Your WiFi section

Devices per Type

The Devices per Type section presents the breakdown of user devices which have contributed to the collection of all data, broken by their type and supplemented with the breakdowns of those devices by:

  • the number of those devices,
  • the total number of sessions established by them,
  • the total data transfer consumed by each type of device,
  • the total dwell time of devices of a given type.

The What Do You Know About Your Network Usage? Section

Connections

The Connections table displays the number of connections to the WiFi which have been made by your users.

How are connections measured?

The system registers a connection when a user completes the login process and gains internet access or logs in automatically to the network. However, each time the user disconnects from the network for a set up minimal time and connects to it again, the system records another connection. As a result, one user can make multiple registered connections.

Total Sessions Duration

The Total Sessions Duration table shows the total duration of all sessions which have been made by the users.

Total Data Transfer

The Total Data Transfer table presents the total amount of data which have been downloaded and uploaded by the users.

Average Connections Count per Hour

The Average Connection Count per Hour bar chart shows the average number of network connections which have been made by the users per hour. To preview the exact amount of connections, hover your mouse over the desired bar

Total Data Download

The Total Data Download table displays the total amount of data which have been downloaded by your WiFi users, in MB.

Total Data Upload

The Total Data Transfer table displays the total amount of data which have been uploaded by your WiFi users, in MB.

Data Transfer

The Data Transfer bar chart presents the daily breakdown of total downloaded and uploaded data for the selected granularity (hour, day, or week). The data transfer is displayed as a sum of downloaded and uploaded data, broken down by these two types. To preview the exact amount of data, hover your mouse over the desired bar.

Exporting the Data

All the graphs can be exported as CSV files. To download them, click the Export to CSV icon in the top right corner of the table and save the file on your computer.
