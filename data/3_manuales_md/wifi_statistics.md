# Find your way around Wi-Fi Users statistics

## About Wi-Fi Users statistics

The Wi-Fi Users statistics panel provides combined footfall data for a selected time frame and is located in **Reporting → Statistics**.

At the top of the panel, you can select the time frame for which you want to see the data. By default, it shows the last seven days.

![date picker](images/stats_calendar.png)

The whole panel is divided into four sections:

* **What do you know about Users' visits?**
* **Which suborganisation do the data come from?**
* **Who are the Users?**
* **What do you know about Connections?**

## Definitions

Throughout the Statistics following terms are used:

**User** - a person who logged in to Wi-Fi,  
**New user** - a User who:

* logged in to Wi-Fi for the first time ever,
* didn’t log in within the preset interval,
* in any type of granularity (hourly, daily, yearly) can be recognized as new and returning at the same time.

  Example

  If a User logs in for the first time one day, even though seen as returning in daily granularity (because of the second login), he is defined as new user for the purpose of statistics.

**Returning user** - a User who is not new and logged in to Wi-Fi within the preset interval,
**Connection** - a connection to a Wi-Fi network,  
**Device** - every device that can connect to Wi-Fi, such as: a mobile phone, a computer, or a smart device. It is identified by a Device ID (usually it is a MAC address of a device).

## What do you know about Users' visits?

This section presents the collected data about Users visits' frequency and information about their preferable time of visit.

### Users

![number of users](images/users.png)  
A total number of Users who logged in within the selected time frame.

Tip

Each User must see a Portal to log in. With the exact data about audience size, it will be much easier for you to attract advertisers and resell the Portal's space.

### New Users

![number of new users](images/new_users.png)  
The number of new Users within the selected time frame.

Tip

Use this data to check effectiveness of your ongoing promotions and marketing campaigns.

### Returning Users

![number of returning users](images/returning_users.png)  
The number of returning Users within the selected time frame.

Tip

Loyal customers are important part of running a business as they regularly spend money in your venue, meet there with their friends, and recommend it to other people. If the number of **Returning Users** is not satisfying, try running a loyalty programme.

### Average User Count per Hour

![average user count](images/average_user.png)  
The bar chart showing an average number of Users grouped by hours and pivoted by User type (new/returning) cumulated into one 24-hour period.

Tip

Use this chart to learn in what hours you may need more staff and adapt operational hours to maximize profit.

### User Count over Time

![user over time](images/user_time.png)  
The bar chart showing a number of Users grouped by hours and pivoted by User type (new/returning) with comparison to an average number for the selected time frame.

### Rush Hours

![rush hours](images/rush_hours.png)
The heat map showing an average number of Users within the selected time frame.

Tip

Use the **User Count over Time** and **Rush Hours** graphs to spot trends. Maybe on one particular day there are more customers than on other days? With this data, you can adapt the number of staff needed per shift.  
Also, you can try redistributing the rush evenly by creating events in off-peak hours.

### Best Hour

![best hour](images/best_hour.png)  
The hour with the highest number of Users within the selected time frame.

### Best Day

![best day](images/best_day.png)

### Best Day on Record

![record day](images/record.png)

With the **Best Hour**, **Best Day**, and **Best Day on Record** data you can try to deduce what attracted customers then and repeat it.

## Which suborganisation do the data come from?

This section shows you the data about the number of visitors in first-level suborganisations.

What is a first level suborganisation?

In the platform, you can reflect the structure of your organisation using a management tree. A first level suborganisation is an organisation that is rooted directly in your organisation. Depending on a tree structure, it can be a whole business, single shop/coffee house, or a city.   
![management tree](images/management_tree.png)  
In this case, the first level organisations are *Krakow*, *London*, and *Madrid*.

### Organisations

![organisation chart](images/organisations.png)  
The pie chart showing first-level suborganisations' contribution to the total data collected.

### Participation rate

![organisation list](images/organisation_list.png)  
A list of all first-level suborganisations with the number of Users and its participation rate in the total data collected.

Tip

Use the **Organisations** and **Participation rate** charts to quickly scan suborganisations' profitability. Maybe one of the coffee shops needs a check to see why the results are so poor? Or perhaps a shop with the best results introduced some great offers that can be spread to other venues?

## What do you know about Connections?

In this section, there are data about technical aspects of Users' visits.

### Connections

![connections](images/connections.png)   
The number of connections to a Wi-Fi network within the selected time frame.

Note

The difference between this and the **User** numbers is the number of people who connected to your Wi-Fi network but didn't go through the log in process.

### Average Connection Count per Hour

![average connections](images/average_connections.png)   
The bar chart showing an average number of connections to Wi-Fi in hourly granularity within the selected time frame.

Tip

Use the **Connections** and **Average Connection Count per Hour** data to analyse why customers didn't go through the login process. Maybe it is too complicated or there is a technical problem?  
Remember, the more people log in, the more data you can collect. Try running a promotion for people who log in!

### Devices

![devices](images/devices.png)   
The pie chart showing the contribution to the total for the devices used to connect to Wi-Fi.

Tip

Did you notice that people with laptops tend to spend more time in venues? They also spend more money there!  
Check also the **Connection Duration** on the **Dashboard**. If it's short, try making your venue more work-friendly: comfier chairs, softer music, more power points.
